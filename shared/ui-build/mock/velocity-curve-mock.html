WHAT YAMAHA DOES

Yamaha splits this in two, and we should copy that split — it is the reason their keyboards feel adjustable without feeling broken.

CURVEA global reshaping of the velocity number itself, before the tone generator sees it. Normal, Soft 1, Soft 2, Hard 1, Hard 2, Wide, Fixed.
DEPTHPer part. How much effort it takes to reach full output. 64 is neutral.
OFFSETPer part. Shifts the whole response up or down. 64 is neutral.
FIXED1–127. Only live when CURVE is Fixed.

WHERE IT LANDS IN OUR ENGINE

MIDI note on, velocity v
  ↓
CURVE — reshapes v  (NEW)
  ↓
layerGains() — which velocity layer
selectZone() — which sample
  ↓
DEPTH / OFFSET — level only  (NEW)
  ↓
v->gain  Sampler.h:390

That ordering is the whole point. CURVE changes which sample you hit — a soft curve genuinely reaches the loud layers sooner. DEPTH and OFFSET only change how loud it is, so you can tame a part without changing its character.

THE MATH

Curve, on the velocity: out = 127 · (v/127)g with g = 0.55 / 0.75 / 1.0 / 1.35 / 1.80 for Soft 1 … Hard 1. Wide is the same law mirrored about the middle, so it pushes soft playing down and hard playing up.

Level, after layer selection: clamp(v · Depth/64 + (Offset − 64), 0, 127)

Honestly: Yamaha does not publish the equation for Depth and Offset. This formula is my reading of the behaviour their own worked examples describe, and it reproduces three of their five published examples exactly. If it should match a specific instrument of James's, we tune the constants by ear against that instrument — they are four numbers in one place.
